Video 2 Deep Dive

Finding Local "Pains"

The best apps solve real problems. Let's use Gemini to find them.

Where to Look for Problems

  • 1 Your daily routine — what annoys you?
  • 2 Your campus — what's inefficient?
  • 3 Your community — what costs people time or money?
  • 4 Your market — what's overpriced or unavailable?
Try This Prompt
I am a university student in [YOUR CITY], Nigeria. What are 10 daily frustrations that students face related to: - Getting around campus and the city - Buying food and essentials - Studying and accessing materials - Managing money For each, explain WHY it's frustrating and HOW OFTEN it happens.

Workshop Exercise

Go Deeper

Pick the ONE problem that excites you most. Now interrogate it with Gemini.

Problem Analysis Prompt
I want to solve this problem: [DESCRIBE THE PROBLEM] Help me understand it deeply: 1. WHO suffers from this the most? (Be specific — age, location, situation) 2. WHY does this problem still exist? What's stopping people from fixing it? 3. HOW do people currently deal with it? (Workarounds, alternatives) 4. WHAT would the perfect solution look like? 5. HOW MUCH would someone pay to have this solved? (Or how much time would they save?)

Key Insight: The best problems to solve are ones where people already spend time or money on bad workarounds. If nobody cares enough to even try a workaround, the problem might not be worth solving.

Define Your MVP

Minimum Viable Product — the simplest version of your app that still solves the problem.

MVP = Only the Essentials

  • ✓ Solves ONE core problem
  • ✓ Has maximum 3 features
  • ✓ Can be built in 2 weeks
  • ✗ NOT a full platform with 20 features
MVP Definition Prompt
I want to build an app that solves: [PROBLEM] Help me define the MVP: 1. App Name (catchy, memorable) 2. One-line description (max 15 words) 3. Target user (specific person) 4. Top 3 features ONLY 5. What it does NOT include (scope limits) 6. How users will access it (phone app via AppSheet)

Evaluation

Is Your Idea Good Enough?

Use this checklist to evaluate your app idea before moving forward.

Strong Idea

  • ✅ You personally experience the problem
  • ✅ 10+ people you know have this problem
  • ✅ People currently waste time/money on it
  • ✅ Can be built with Google tools

Needs Work

  • ⚠️ Only you have this problem
  • ⚠️ Too many features planned
  • ⚠️ Unclear who the user is
  • ⚠️ Similar free solutions exist

Restart

  • ❌ Can't explain it in one sentence
  • ❌ Needs complex technology
  • ❌ No one would pay or save time
  • ❌ You're not excited about it

Video 3 Exercise

Craft Your Elevator Pitch

30 seconds. 4 sentences. Make someone care about your idea.

Pitch Formula
Help me write a 30-second elevator pitch using this formula: 1. THE PROBLEM: "[TARGET USERS] struggle with [PROBLEM] every day." 2. THE PAIN: "This costs them [TIME/MONEY/STRESS]." 3. THE SOLUTION: "[APP NAME] solves this by [HOW IT WORKS]." 4. THE HOOK: "Imagine [BENEFIT — paint a picture]." Make it conversational, not corporate. Use simple English.
Weak Pitch
"My app is a platform that leverages AI to optimize the textbook acquisition process for educational stakeholders."
Strong Pitch
"Students at UNILAG spend 3 hours hunting for affordable textbooks every semester. BookSwap lets you buy and sell used textbooks on campus in 2 taps. No more overpriced bookshops."

Workshop Activity

Peer Review

Share your pitch with a partner. Use this framework to give feedback.

When Listening to a Pitch

  • 1 Can you explain the idea back in your own words?
  • 2 Would YOU use this app? Why or why not?
  • 3 What's the most exciting part?
  • 4 What's unclear or confusing?
  • 5 Who else do you know that has this problem?

Improve with Gemini

I got this feedback on my pitch: [PASTE FEEDBACK] My current pitch: [YOUR PITCH] Help me improve the pitch based on this feedback. Keep it under 4 sentences.
